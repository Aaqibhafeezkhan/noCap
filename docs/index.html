What it answers

Questions you used to answer by ear.

Flow is the hardest part of rap to put into words. noCap turns it into something you can point at.

Density

How dense is the flow?

Every bar is a row, every beat a column. Syllables land exactly where they were rapped, so double-time runs, triplets and breathing room are obvious at a glance.

  • Syllables per beat, per bar and per section
  • Peak and average density, bar-by-bar ranking
  • Syncopation score — how often you land off the grid
Pocket

Ahead, on, or behind the beat?

noCap measures each syllable's offset from the grid, then summarizes it as pocket and timing quality — the difference between laid-back and rushing, in numbers.

  • Pocket score with average and range of offsets
  • Timing variance and delivery consistency
  • Compare bars by density, sync, stress, pocket or timing
Rhymes

Where do the rhymes land?

Phonetic matching groups perfect, slant, assonance and internal rhymes, then color-codes them on the grid. Follow a rhyme chain across bars and see how the scheme is built.

  • Multi-syllable and internal rhyme detection
  • Click a group to highlight it across the whole track
  • Average rhyme-chain length as a metric
Details

Break it down, section by section.

The Details view puts every metric in one place and splits the track into sections, each with its own density, syncopation and tempo. Click any syllable on the grid to hear exactly what you're looking at.

  • Click-to-play with original mix or vocals only
  • Section table with density, sync, onset and tempo
  • Export the flow map as PNG or SVG

How it works

From audio file to flow map in four steps.

  1. 01

    Drop a track

    MP3, WAV, FLAC, AIFF, M4A or OGG. Beat grid and BPM are detected from the waveform.

  2. 02

    Isolate the vocals

    Demucs separates the vocal stem so the beat doesn't confuse transcription.

  3. 03

    Transcribe every word

    Whisper produces word-level timestamps, refined to roughly 10 ms accuracy.

  4. 04

    Snap to the grid

    Words are split into syllables with stress from the CMU dictionary and placed on the beat grid.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is it really free?

Yes. noCap is MIT-licensed open source. There's no account, subscription or usage limit.

Does my audio get uploaded anywhere?

No. Beat detection, vocal separation and transcription all run on your Mac. Tracks and analyses are stored locally in your library.

Which Macs are supported? What about Windows or Linux?

The current release is a self-contained build for Apple Silicon Macs. On Intel Macs, Windows or Linux you can run noCap from source today — see the developer setup. Prebuilt packages for more platforms are welcome as contributions.

Why is the download so large?

The app bundles everything it needs — Python, PyTorch, Demucs and the Whisper model — so it works offline with no setup. The trade-off is a big one-time download.

macOS says the app can't be opened. What do I do?

Right-click (or Control-click) noCap in Applications and choose Open, then confirm. You only need to do this once.

Does it work with lyrics in languages other than English?

noCap uses Whisper's English model and the CMU (English) pronouncing dictionary, so English-language tracks give the best results today. Multilingual support is on the wish list.

How long does an analysis take?

It depends on track length and your machine; separation and transcription are the heavy steps. Once analyzed, a track reopens instantly from your library.
